## Task
One rack of American 8-ball on a 7-ft pool table: break, then call every shot (a ball and a pocket, or safety), clear your group (solids or stripes) and pot the 8 into the pocket you call; you shoot by cue direction, cue speed and tip offset, and place the cue ball when you have ball in hand; in the `eightball` format the opponent is a scripted computer player, in the `duel` format another player; format `duel` (seats A, B),
action level `low`, program play. Generated by `tools/generate.py` from
`sports/billiards/sport.toml`; do not edit by hand.

## Difficulty
Set at run time: the seed (`RO_SEED`; it draws the match's random conditions) and the first player (`FIRST_PLAYER`; play both orders).

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` runs the sport's reference player (`solution/builtin_players.py`) through the core's client
(`solution/ro_client.py`): camera images and `/spec` only.

## Verification
The `sim` service (the v0.2 core, `environment/sim/core/`) records the match (`episode.jsonl`) and a fresh process
replays it (state hash); the verifier (`tests/grade.py` = `core/grading.py`, no network) writes the common reward
fields (`success`, `valid_trial`, `deterministic`, `<seat>_won`, ...) and the sport's fields (`tests/sport_grade.py`).

| Rules | [WPA Rules of Play, section 4 (8-Ball) with the general rules (section 1) and fouls (section 3)](https://wpapool.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025.09.15-WPA-Rules.pdf) version effective 2025-09-15 |
| id | rule | deviation |
| D1 | 4.3(e), 4.3(f) | the 8 pocketed on the break is always spotted and the breaker continues if the break was legal (no re-break option) |
| D2 | 4.3(d) | after an illegal break the other player always re-racks the same rack and breaks (option (2); the other options do not exist) |
| D3 | 4.3(g), 4.3(h) | a foul on the break gives the other player ball in hand above the head string (no option to accept the table) |
| D4 | 1.6 | with ball in hand above the head string you cannot ask for an object ball above it to be spotted |
| D5 | 1.7, 4.6 | every shot after the break is called explicitly (a ball and a pocket, or safety): no obvious shots |
| D6 | 2.9 | the action has no cue elevation, so there are no jump or masse shots |
| D7 | 1.6 | the cue ball cannot be placed touching a ball or in a pocket opening |
| D8 | 4.11 | a 60-shot limit per player replaces the stalemate rule: a player out of shots passes; when both have used all 60 the player with fewer balls left in its group wins (an open table counts as 7); if level, the player with more chess-clock time left (eightball: the scripted opponent's clock never runs, so a tie goes to it); if the clocks are equal too, (last resort, not from the rulebook) the player who did not break |
| D9 | 1.2, 4.1 | no lag for the break (the rulebook: the players lag and the winner of the lag chooses who breaks; neither the Rules of Play nor the Playing Regulations provide a coin toss): duel: a coin toss drawn from the seed decides who breaks (FIRST_PLAYER if it falls 0, else the other player; the toss decides, nobody chooses), and every pairing plays both first orders; eightball: the configuration decides (level 1 you, level 2 the scripted opponent) |
| D10 | none (simulation) | a shot the physics engine cannot resolve (a rare internal failure of pooltool) is simulated again from the same position with the aim nudged by 0.001 degree steps (+, -, up to 0.01) until it resolves; the shot report says so (physics_retry) |
| D11 | section 1 (the player shoots with the cue) | the cue is a declared cue machine, like Deep Green, the pool-playing robot of Queen's University (a ceiling-mounted gantry robot with a cue on a robotic wrist; Greenspan et al., Toward a Competitive Pool-Playing Robot, IEEE Computer 41(1), 46-53, 2008, https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2008.33), not a player: you set the cue's direction, speed and tip offset at impact and the machine strikes with a stated execution scatter. Cue speed 0.3 to 7 m/s: within the cue speeds reported for Deep Green (about 3 m/s for its normal stroke, up to 12 m/s for its break stroke; speeds above about 3 m/s correspond to the break stroke); the execution scatter is an estimate, not the machine's number |
