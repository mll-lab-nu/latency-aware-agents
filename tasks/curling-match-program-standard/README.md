## Task
Two teams slide granite stones down a sheet of ice towards the house, 4 ends of 5 stones per team; you skip one team and throw every one of its stones (release speed, direction and spin); in the `match` format the other team is a scripted computer team, in the `duel` format another player; format `match` (seats A),
action level `low`, program play. Generated by `tools/generate.py` from
`sports/curling/sport.toml`; do not edit by hand.

## Difficulty
Set at run time: the seed (`RO_SEED`; it draws the match's random conditions).

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` runs the sport's reference player (`solution/builtin_players.py`) through the core's client
(`solution/ro_client.py`): camera images and `/spec` only.

## Verification
The `sim` service (the v0.2 core, `environment/sim/core/`) records the match (`episode.jsonl`) and a fresh process
replays it (state hash); the verifier (`tests/grade.py` = `core/grading.py`, no network) writes the common reward
fields (`success`, `valid_trial`, `deterministic`, `<seat>_won`, ...) and the sport's fields (`tests/sport_grade.py`).

## Details
| | |
|---|---|
| Rules | [World Curling, The Rules of Curling and Rules of Competition](https://worldcurling.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Rules-2025.pdf) version July 2025 |
| id | rule | deviation |
| D1 | C5, R2(b) | 4 ends of 5 stones per team (official: 8 or 10 ends of 8 stones) |
| D2 | R12(a) | at most 3 extra ends while tied; then the player with more chess-clock time left wins (official: extra ends until a team scores) |
| D3 | R8 | no sweeping |
| D4 | R5 | the stone is released at the hack with the given speed, direction and spin (no delivery rules) |
| D5 | R7 | no no-tick rule |
| D6 | R5(a) | match format: the scripted team has the hammer in end 1 (a fixed handicap; official: coin toss or last stone draw); duel: a coin toss drawn from the seed decides who throws the first stone of end 1 (FIRST_PLAYER if it falls 0, else the other player; it falls 0 for the official seeds 1-3); the other player has the hammer in end 1 |
| D7 | R5 (delivery) | the stones are thrown by a declared delivery machine, like the real curling robots: Sone and Kawamura's pneumatic stone delivery robot (Shinshu University; J. Robot. Mechatron. 36(4), 864-870, 2024, https://doi.org/10.20965/jrm.2024.p0864) delivers at 2.0 to 4.0 m/s with high repeatability, and Curly (Korea University; Won, Mueller and Lee, Science Robotics 5(46), eabb9764, 2020, https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abb9764) won 3 of 4 official matches against top-level human teams. Release speed 0.5 to 4.0 m/s: the top is the Sone machine's; below about 2.2 m/s a stone stops short of the far hog line and is removed from play, so every throw that stays in play is within the machine's 2.0 to 4.0 m/s. The spin (always pi/2 rad/s, either way) is within the human range but has no robot source, and the execution scatter is an estimate, not a machine's number |
